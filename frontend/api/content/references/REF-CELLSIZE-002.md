Normocytic anaemia presents an extensive diagnostic challenge because erythrocyte dimensions remain within expected reference boundaries. The crucial immediate discriminator is the reticulocyte response. An elevated reticulocyte index indicates functional marrow response to acute haemorrhage or peripheral haemolysis. Conversely, a low or inappropriate reticulocyte response indicates hypoproliferation, common in renal failure, endocrine insufficiency, bone marrow infiltration, or anaemia of chronic disease.

Importantly, normocytic indices can conceal combined nutritional deficiencies. When iron deficiency coexists with vitamin B12 or folate deficiency, microcytic and macrocytic cell lines balance each other, generating a composite MCV within typical limits. Scrutinizing the blood film and RDW clarifies this pseudo-normal presentation. Typical adult reference ranges vary by laboratory.
