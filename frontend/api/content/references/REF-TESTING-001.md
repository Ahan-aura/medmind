Modern clinical guidelines advocate structured diagnostic cascades rather than indiscriminate laboratory ordering. When anaemia is identified, testing should be prioritized strictly by morphological phenotype. In microcytic anaemia, obtaining serum ferritin represents the initial high-yield investigation. Reflexively bundling haemoglobin electrophoresis, autoimmune panels, and abdominal imaging before ferritin is verified generates wasteful expenditure and potential diagnostic confusion.

Similarly, in macrocytic anaemia, initial blood film inspection alongside serum B12 and folate levels provides high diagnostic yield. Advanced testing—such as bone marrow aspiration or specialised haemoglobin analysis—should be reserved for cases where initial first-tier non-invasive investigations fail to reveal the diagnosis or when multilineage dysplasia is present. Typical adult reference ranges vary by laboratory.
