Serum ferritin is the most specific non-invasive laboratory marker of depleted iron reserves. In uncomplicated individuals without systemic inflammation, a subnormal ferritin level is diagnostic of exhausted iron stores. However, ferritin synthesis is heavily up-regulated by inflammatory cytokines including interleukin-6 and tumour necrosis factor during acute infection, systemic rheumatologic conditions, chronic kidney disease, hepatic inflammation, and neoplasia.

Consequently, in the setting of chronic inflammation, a patient with absolute iron exhaustion can exhibit a serum ferritin value well within or above the standard reference interval. In such complex cases, clinician interpretation must integrate inflammatory markers, transferrin saturation, and clinical context rather than accepting normal ferritin as proof of iron adequacy. Typical adult reference ranges vary by laboratory.
